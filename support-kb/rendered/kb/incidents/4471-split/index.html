The three problems

parser: the shared parser reads dates month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so 7 April becomes 4 July. This covers reports from every tenant, through the app, email and the booking-v2 form.

hint: booking-v3 tells day-first tenants to type the month first. Its date field prints MM/DD/YYYY. People follow the hint, and the form reads what they type day first. This covers only reports sent through the booking form of a booking-v3 tenant. Fixing the parser does not fix it.

one-day move: the date moves by one day, not by a swap. The month stays and the day shifts by one, and it depends on where the reader is (“Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday.”). This looks like a time-zone conversion, not the parser. The desk had already marked these reports as one-day moves (desk-notes.json). The desk files a report by its tenant, so they stay counted under parser too, footnoted under the table.

Outcome

  • parser: fixed, fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. Ticket closed as completed.
  • hint: won’t fix, because the booking-v3 form is retired. Ticket closed as not planned.
  • one-day move: closed with #4471 as not planned. Neither change touches it.

Why one ticket hid three

  • Decision 0001 merges reports on subject line alone. All three problems produce the same subjects (“Wrong day on the invite”, “Month and day swapped?”), so they were merged into one ticket.
  • The intake tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser, not the tenant’s locale and form. It disagrees with the cause on many reports (the column above), so counting by tag is wrong too.
  • The ticket export repeats some reports across pages. Counted once by id.

For the desk

  • A day/month swap reported after 5 October is a regression of the parser fix: open a new ticket.
  • Send reports of a date off by one day to the one-day-move ticket and reopen it, whatever their subject says.
